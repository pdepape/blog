Documentation measurement

How to measure documentation quality

Good metrics explain what readers tried to do, where they struggled, and whether a documentation change improved the outcome. Page views alone cannot do that.

Published October 6, 2026 by TechDocs Studio

Use a balanced scorecard rather than one headline number. Select a small set of metrics from each layer below, define the decision each metric informs, and record a baseline before changing the content.

Findability metrics

  • Search impressions, clicks, and click-through rate for priority queries.
  • Internal search success, refinements, and zero-result searches.
  • Entry pages used for critical reader journeys.
  • Navigation paths and exits before the first task step.

These metrics answer whether readers can reach the right page using their language.

Task-success metrics

  • Completion rate for a documented setup or integration.
  • Time to first successful outcome.
  • Error and retry rate at documented steps.
  • Escalation to support after visiting a guide.

Instrument the product outcome when possible. A guide is successful when the reader completes the job, not simply when the page receives a visit.

Accuracy and trust metrics

  • Percentage of critical examples tested against the current product.
  • Confirmed defects per 100 reviewed pages.
  • Age of unresolved documentation bugs.
  • Reader reports that identify inaccurate or missing information.

Maintenance metrics

  • Critical pages with a named owner and review trigger.
  • Documentation changes merged before or with the related release.
  • Median time from product change to documentation update.
  • Pages beyond their expected review interval.

Business and support signals

Connect documentation to product outcomes carefully. Compare support contacts, onboarding completion, activation, or integration success for well-defined journeys. Avoid claiming causation when several product or campaign changes occurred at the same time.

A compact monthly scorecard

  1. Discovery: priority-query clicks and internal-search success.
  2. Completion: success rate for one critical journey.
  3. Accuracy: open high-severity documentation defects.
  4. Freshness: percentage of critical pages within review interval.
  5. Feedback: top unresolved reader problem and its owner.
